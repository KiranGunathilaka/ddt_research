One lidar or two: field of view in a tight hairpin

3 November 2026 · Falcon FS-AI DDT entry on the IMechE ADS-DV · follows on from Sensors and Autonomy Stack

More than one lidarTrack rules, to scale2D and 3D modelWhat the code doesWho runs 120° aloneTeams that added a sensorFor Falcon

A 120° solid-state lidar gives a dense, long-range view ahead of the car but sees nothing to the sides. Falcon already owns a Velodyne VLP-16, which spins through 360°. That raises two questions: has any team run more than one lidar, and how do teams with a single 120° lidar get through the tightest hairpin the rules allow? This page answers both from the FSG team pages and from the teams' published code. It checks the track rules line by line and draws them to scale. Then it puts a Hesai ATX (120° × 20°) and the VLP-16 on the ADS-DV in a 2D and 3D model of those tracks.

Multiple lidars made sense when each one was sparse: 4-layer ibeo LUX and SICK LD-MRS units, or 16-channel Pucks. Once 64- and 128-channel spinners and 120° solid-state units became affordable, almost every team moved to one. The grid shows each team that ever listed more than one lidar.   • Closest to our idea: Revolve NTNU in 2019 ran an Ouster OS-1 together with a Velodyne VLP-16, two different models on one car (FSG). Elbflorace’s three Livox HAPs cover the sides of a forward solid-state view, which is the same goal (FSG).
  • Merging raw point clouds is expensive. KIT merged four ibeo LUX clouds before detection. They built a sync ECU that pulses all four lidars, synchronised it to the main computer with PTP over CAN, and matched scans by counter because Ethernet latency varied (Nekkah 2020, p. 3).
  • AMZ’s 2019 car had two 32-channel lidars and two cameras, all time-synchronised to a common GNSS clock (Andresen 2020, p. 3).
  • No FS-AI DDT team on the ADS-DV is known to have used two lidars; EUFS, IIT Bombay and Hydrakon each ran one 16-beam lidar (Sensors and Autonomy Stack).

  • The track geometry is the same. Hairpins, width, straights, constant turns and turn sequences are word for word the same in FS-AI and in FSUK’s driverless trackdrive, in 2026 and in 2027.
  • FS-AI leaves out two FSUK lines: slaloms and track lengths. FSUK’s Driverless Cup drives autocross and trackdrive on one track; in FS-AI the trackdrive layout may differ.
  • The 5 m cone spacing is FS-AI’s own, from its cones specification. The FSUK rulebook has no such line.
  • Nothing sets a gap between turns, so two minimum hairpins back to back are legal.
  • Corners need not get extra cones. The 5 m maximum holds in corners too, and closer spacing there is allowed, not required. Spaced 5 m apart, the inside edge of an 11 m hairpin, a half circle 5 m across, can be marked by its first and last cone alone.
  • Autocross is one lap with no data from earlier runs, so the cone map has to be built during that lap.

Drawn to scale

Each drawing is to scale and has a scale bar. The ADS-DV is drawn at the same scale, from the coarse outline of the official CAD that the ADS-DV Explorer uses. Cones are drawn at their real 228 mm base (cones specification, Table 2, p. 2).

The ADS-DV does not fit the minimum hairpin. The AI can request at most ±21° of road-wheel angle (ADS-DV Explorer, SIS p. 4). With a 1.533 m wheelbase, the tightest the rear axle can turn is a 3.99 m radius. Then the outer front tyre sweeps 4.95 m from the turn centre, a kerb-to-kerb turning circle of 9.9 m. A 180° turn needs that much room between the outside cones, plus a cone’s half-width each side: 10.1 m. The rules allow 9 m. So in a 9 m hairpin the ADS-DV touches the outside cones on any line. It clears an 11 m hairpin by 0.43 m. This is a kinematic estimate with no tyre slip; a slipping car runs wider still.

A Hesai ATX and the VLP-16 on the ADS-DV, in 2D and 3D

The 2D and 3D views below share one model. Pick a track, or draw one under Your track, then move the car and pick the ATX, the VLP-16 or both.

  • Car: ADS-DV, 1.533 m wheelbase, steering limited to the ±21° the AI can request. The rear axle follows the track centreline; in the 11 m hairpin that is full lock. The tracks: an 11 m hairpin, two 11 m hairpins back to back (legal under “multiple turns”), and the skidpad to the rules. Cones are placed as the cones specification asks: at most 5 m apart on straights, closer in corners.
  • Hesai ATX, the 120° lidar we would buy: 120° × 20°, 256 channels, 0.08° in azimuth and 3.84 million points a second, which is 256 × 1500 rays at 10 Hz (Hesai). ISC Racing already runs one at ±60° (below). The model spreads the 256 channels evenly over ±10°, about 0.08° apart, and mounts the unit level; the real ATX packs them closer near the middle, 0.05° at the finest. It sits on the front sensor plate, 425 mm ahead of the wheelbase midpoint (CAD, ADS-DV Explorer), 0.57 m up, the lidar height in the 2026 CarMaker model (DDT Architecture and Tests).
  • VLP-16: on a short post above it, 0.70 m up. It has 16 channels 2° apart from −15° to +15°, scans every 0.2° in azimuth, and loses the rear 90° to the car body.
  • Rings on the ground: both views draw, to scale, where each channel pointing down meets the ground, out to 45 m, broken where a cone or the car is in the way. The VLP-16’s 8 rings fall 2.6, 3.0, 3.6, 4.4, 5.7, 8.0, 13.4 and 40 m out. The ATX’s 119 rings are drawn faint, as at 0.08° apart they would fill the view.
  • The car in its own way: rays that hit the ADS-DV first are dropped, using the bodywork outline from the CAD and round front tyres.
  • Seeing a cone: every ray is cast against the ground and against each cone, modelled as a 228 mm-base, 325 mm-tall frustum. Nearer cones hide farther ones. A cone counts as seen when at least 3 rays hit it.
  • Your track: drawn with the mouse. Each turn is a peg the track wraps round, set by its outside diameter, and the straights run from peg to peg. Every edit is checked against the track rules before it is drawn: straights up to 80 m, turns of 9 to 50 m outside diameter, and a track at least 3 m wide (FS-AI D6.1.1, p. 32, D8.1.1, p. 33). Both diameter limits are held for every turn, though the rules write the 9 m one for hairpins and the 50 m one for constant turns. Turns may sit right against each other, as in a chicane or a tightening turn; the only limit there is geometry, since a smooth line must still join them. Parts of the track may also cross or overlap, since the rules say nothing about how close two parts may come. Where they cross, no cone stands on the other road and one stands at each end of the opening, so the gap that leaves must still be 5 m at most (DE6.3.1, p. 1). On a 3 m track two straights have to cross at about 37° or more, and a track wider than 5 m cannot cross at all. The page notes a crossing, and two parts whose cone rows come within 1 m, without stopping either. Real layouts never cross: none of the 14 competition tracks that ship with PacSim crosses itself, and the closest two separate parts come is 2.1 m between cones, at FSE23 and FSI24 (PacSim tracks). The one figure of eight is the skidpad, a separate event with a fixed layout (FS-AI D4.1.1, p. 30). Cones go by default in the worst case the cones specification allows: exactly 5 m apart everywhere, corners included, measured in a straight line (DE6.3.1, p. 1). The other choice puts them closer in corners, as on the tracks above.
  • Map and planner: every cone seen stays in the map at its true place. The planner takes either the map cones within 25 m ahead of the rear axle, as ISC does, or only the current scan. An edge with no partner cone gets a virtual cone 3 m across, as in FaSTTUBe’s planner.

Drag the track into shape on the map. Each turn is a peg the track wraps round, set by its outside diameter, the measure the rules use for hairpins. The straights run from peg to peg. Cones go in the worst case the rules allow unless you pick otherwise. An edit that would break a rule stops at the limit: that rule turns red, and the map shows in red the shape you tried.

Start from

  • Move a turn: drag its numbered centre, or the track where it bends.
  • Change its diameter: drag the small handle on its ring, in 0.5 m steps.
  • Add a turn: drag a straight sideways. Turn the other way: double-click the centre. Remove: select it, then Delete.
  • Cross the track over itself: drag a turn across another part. No cone stands on the other road, and the gap that leaves must be 5 m or less, so the parts have to cross fairly squarely.
  • Map: drag empty ground to pan; scroll or pinch to zoom. Click a cone, here or in the top view below, to knock it down or put it back. Knocked-down cones are not replaced during a run (FS-AI D8.2.9, p. 34).

      What the model shows

      • 11 m hairpin, ATX alone: all 7 inside cones are seen on the approach, from the start of the model run 24 m before turn-in, until 0.8–5.0 m before turn-in. Through the turn none is seen. All of them are in the map at turn-in, so a planner that reads map cones ahead has the whole inside edge.
      • Planner on the current scan only: at the apex it sees just the outside cones, and the virtual cones 3 m across land on the inside edge, because the track is the 3 m minimum. On a wider track they land inside the track: safe, but slower.
      • Two hairpins back to back: the second hairpin is easier to see. Its inside cones are seen on the approach to the first hairpin and again while the car swings round the first one, last 0.3–1.8 m before the second turn-in. All are in the map before the car needs them. The model flips the steering from full left to full right at the join; the real car needs a short distance to unwind, so it runs wide there.
      • Skidpad: on the 9.125 m centreline radius the ATX keeps 3 or 4 inside cones of the circle in view the whole way round.
      • VLP-16 alone: 4 to 7 of the inside cones are in view at every point of each hairpin, but it sees less far ahead: it first sees the 11 m hairpin’s inside cones 11–13 m before turn-in, against 24 m (the whole run) for the ATX. On the skidpad 11 of the 16 inside cones of each circle are in the map as the car enters it, against 15 and 16 with the ATX.
      • Both: the ATX’s reach and the VLP-16’s side view; 4 to 7 inside cones in view through each hairpin.
      • Worst legal cone placing (Your track, started from Hairpin, 11 m): the 11 m hairpin keeps 1 cone on its inside edge instead of 7, and 24 cones in all instead of 36. In a 9 m hairpin, depending on where the 5 m spacing falls, no cone is left between turn-in and exit in about two cases out of three. The ATX sees the one cone until 2.0 m before turn-in, so it is in the map. Through the turn the planner fills in the rest of the inside edge with up to 4 virtual cones. That is the case FaSTTUBe’s planner was written for, and the one to test on the rig.
      • Range and the 20° vertical view: the ATX puts 242 rays on a cone 10 m ahead, 60 at 20 m, 28 at 30 m and 6 at 60 m. Within about 1.4 m the top of a cone is under the lowest beam (−10° from 0.57 m), so a cone that close is not seen at all; the VLP-16’s limit is also 1.4 m. Off to the sides the car itself gets in the way: the front tyres and the bulges of the nose block 6% of the ATX’s rays, all pointing down and to the side. Straight ahead the first ground return is 3.2 m out; at 45° off-centre it is 4.0 m, and at 60° it is 7.5 m. The VLP-16 puts 3 or more rays on a cone out to 10–13 m; farther out, cones fall between its 2° rings.

      What the 120° teams’ code does

      Code for four relevant teams is in the collection:

      • FaSTTUBe (TU Berlin) runs a Livox HAP, 120° (FSG). Their path planner dates from 2021–23, when the lidar was a Livox Horizon with an 82° view.
      • ISC Racing Team (Madrid) runs a Hesai ATX at ±60°. Their 2026 pipeline has SLAM, planning and control.
      • Carnegie Mellon Racing runs a Hesai AT128, 120° (perception docs).
      • Elbflorace (TU Dresden): only their simulator, PacSim, whose example config has a front lidar and two side lidars.
      1. Map the inside cones on the approach. Nothing in the code does this on purpose; it falls out of the geometry above.
      2. Keep the map, and let it grow at corner exits. In a live test on 12 May 2026, ISC’s SLAM read the burst of new cones swinging into view during the first turn as a localisation failure. It stopped using the lidar, drifted on the IMU alone, and the car went off track. The fix: flag a failure only when no known cone matches at all (cone_graph_slam_node.py:115-133).
      3. Plan on map cones, not the scan. FaSTTUBe’s planner takes “the position of the (optionally colored) cones in the slam map” (README.md:65-69). ISC feeds it every map cone within 25 m that lies ahead of the car, a 180° half-plane, wider than the lidar (fasttube_adapter.py:242-269).
      4. Make up an edge that is still missing. The planner “can very robustly handle one side of the track not being visible, for example the inside of a corner” (README.md:61). Each edge is sorted on its own. A cone with no partner within 5 m and 50° gets a virtual cone 3 m across (config.py:118-130, functional_cone_matching.py:178-192). ISC dropped its own planner for this reason: it “struggled at one-sided observation regions” (fasttube_adapter.py:8-22).
        The catch: on tight turns the made-up edge can run 30+ m past the last real cone. ISC cuts the published path at 12 m so the controller never aims into it (fasttube_adapter.py:53-62).
      5. Slow down. ISC caps lateral acceleration at 3 m/s², so the speed target drops for any radius under 3 m. They also lowered the pure-pursuit minimum look-ahead to 1 m after the car overshot the apex of the first hairpin (control_node.py:324-340).

      Code checked that has nothing on hairpins: PWR Wrocław’s thesis (perception only, repo), Delft’s small sim playground (repo), München UAS’s 2018–19 SLAM, written before their 120° lidar (repo), and Trento’s camera-only ORB-SLAM (repo).

      What this means for Falcon

      A single 120° lidar is enough for the tightest hairpin the ADS-DV can drive, and for two of them back to back, if the software does the work the 120° teams’ code does. The VLP-16 then becomes insurance rather than a requirement.

      A 120° lidar alone needs, in our stack
      • Cone SLAM that keeps every cone and treats new cones swinging into view at a corner exit as normal.
      • A path planner fed map cones within about 25 m ahead of the car, not the current scan.
      • Virtual cones for an unseen edge. FaSTTUBe’s planner is MIT-licensed (LICENSE) and ISC uses it unchanged. Cut the published path at about 12 m.
      • A corner speed limit from lateral acceleration, and a short minimum look-ahead for the controller.
      Adding the VLP-16 costs
      • Time sync: the VLP-16 takes PPS and NMEA time from the F9P (Sensors and Autonomy Stack). If the 120° unit syncs over PTP, the PC must also run a PTP master disciplined by the F9P.
      • Mounting: both lidars on the one front sensor plate, inside the sensor envelope; shared cars may not use the low zone ahead of the front tyres (DDT Architecture and Tests). Each blocks part of the other’s view, so check it in the CAD first.
      • Calibration: lidar to lidar, and each to the ZED2.
      • Network: a small switch or a second Ethernet port.
      • Power and approval: both share the 12 V, 5 A sensor feed; add the second lidar to the power budget in ddt_essential_costs.xlsx and to the IMechE request in the ASF.

      Recommendation. Build the stack for one 120° lidar. Detect cones separately in each lidar and give SLAM both cone lists, rather than merging point clouds, so the VLP-16 can be added or left off without code changes. On the stage 2a rig, set up a 9 m hairpin with cones 5 m apart, corners included, and push through it with and without the VLP-16. Compare the inside-edge error in the map. Keep it on the car only if that error drops clearly. The other route is a single 360° 32-channel lidar, which covers the hairpin with one sensor and none of the costs above.

      Steering limit. At the ±21° the AI can request, the ADS-DV cannot get round a 9 m hairpin without touching the outside cones (above). Expect FS-AI hairpins to be larger in practice. Measure the real lock on the car, since the HiL VCU reports ±30° (DDT Architecture and Tests). Give the planner the car’s true minimum radius, so a tight turn is driven wide on purpose, not by accident.
