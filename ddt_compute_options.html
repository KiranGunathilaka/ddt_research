What the computer has to do, and where it has to fit

Run our stackVLP-16 on Ethernet, ZED2 on USB 3, IMU and F9P on USB, the car on CAN. A YOLO cone detector, SLAM, and the planner and controller at 50–100 Hz. The ZED SDK’s depth and tracking need an NVIDIA GPU.
Run off the carThe compute plug gives 13.7 V nominal, 30 A fused, shared with the sensor plate. A 7W2 combo D-sub carries the power and the vehicle CAN (500 kbit/s, already terminated). Teams supply their own CAN interface. The sensor socket on the same 12 V system is quoted at 11.5–14.5 V.
Fit the carAt the rear, about 280 × 200 × 200 mm and at most 8 kg including the mounting plate; IMechE suggests anti-vibration mounts. It can also go on the front sensor plate, which has the same plug.
Have a fallbackEvery shared car can carry an InCarPC: i7-8700T, GTX 1060, 32 GB, PEAK CAN, and a removable 2.5-inch SSD caddy. It is shared between teams, so we want our own, but our image should also boot on it.
Mind the old GPUTensorRT 10 needs an NVIDIA GPU of SM 7.5 or newer, and CUDA 13 dropped Pascal. The InCarPC’s GTX 1060 is SM 6.1, so a detector built for an RTX card will not run on it unchanged. Keep a fallback build, and test it before the event.

Why the i7-14700F

The CPU’s power is fixed by the car PSU, not by the chip: the power budget gives it 65 W. At a fixed 65 W, a chip with more cores runs each core at a lower clock and voltage, so it gets more work done for the same power. Every chip below fits the same board, DDR4 RAM and cooler.

  • Speed. At the same 65 W the i7-14700F should be 1.7 to 2 times the i5-12400F in multi-core work, the range set by the two Raptor Lake chips it sits between. That is close to the 24-core i9 for LKR 60,000 less.
  • Cost. Only the CPU line changes: LKR 70,500 more, so the PC is LKR 0.60 M and the essential list LKR 1.00 M. A Ryzen or Core Ultra chip would also need a new mini-ITX board and DDR5 RAM.
  • Used by FSG teams. Prague CTU and Lisboa IST list an i7-14700, and Ghent an i7-14700T (FSG spec sheets, in the cost workbook’s FSG x86 teams sheet).
  • Board BIOS. The H610M-ITX/ac needs BIOS 9.01 or later for a 14th-gen i7 (ASRock’s CPU list, as mirrored by Pangoly and indexed by search). A board with an older BIOS will not start with this CPU until an older chip is fitted to update it, so ask the seller for the BIOS version before ordering.
  • Microcode. Intel traced the 13th- and 14th-gen instability to a clock circuit that ages under high voltage, and its 0x12B microcode stops the high voltage requests (Tom’s Hardware). Flash the newest ASRock BIOS before the first boot of the stack. Intel added two years to the warranty of boxed 13th- and 14th-gen desktop chips, five years in total (PCWorld), and Chama’s in-stock unit is the boxed one.

Fitting it to the ADS-DV

  • Power. The M4-ATX takes the car’s 13.7 V from pins A1 (+12 V) and A2 (0 V) of the compute plug and makes a regulated 12 V rail: 12 A continuous, 16 A peak, between 11 and 16 V input (M4-ATX manual). That rail feeds the board, the CPU and whatever the card draws through its slot, so set the i7-14700F to PL1 = PL2 = 65 W in the BIOS: at its stock 219 W turbo the CPU alone would be above the rail’s 144 W. The card’s 8-pin lead runs from its own 12 V regulator, because the rail cannot also carry a 130 W card. Above 160 W in total the manual asks for forced airflow, so put a fan on the enclosure.
  • CAN. Pins 1 (CAN-H) and 2 (CAN-L) go to the CANable. The car’s bus is already terminated, so leave any terminator on our side off.
  • Size. A mini-ITX board is 170 × 170 mm and the card is 189 mm long and two slots wide, so with the card upright in its slot the PC fits the 280 × 200 × 200 mm space. Build a small aluminium enclosure on the rear plate, on anti-vibration mounts, and check it against IMechE’s rear compute STEP file.
  • Rig. The same PC runs on the rig from the 12 V 18 Ah battery through the same M4-ATX, so the rig also tests the car’s power path.
  • Software. Ubuntu 22.04 with ROS 2 Humble (what the official HiL repository’s ROS documentation assumes), one Docker image for this PC and the InCarPC, and a detector build that runs on the GTX 1060.
  • Upgrade path. The card already has its own regulator, so a faster card with one 8-pin lead, such as the RTX 5060 (LKR 186,500 at Chama Computers on 19 October), can use it; check its power against the power budget sheet first.

The Xavier you can borrow

You are right that it does not run JetPack 6: NVIDIA says JetPack 6 does not support the Xavier series (NVIDIA forum), so it stays on JetPack 5.1.x with Ubuntu 20.04, CUDA 11.4 and TensorRT 8.5. That is old but not useless. ROS 2 Humble runs in a Docker container on it, the ZED SDK has JetPack 5 builds, and its 9–20 V input runs straight off 12 V. Zurich UAS Racing raced almost our exact kit on one: a VLP-16, a ZED 2i and an SBG Ellipse-D on a Jetson AGX Xavier (Finn 2025).

Use it on the rig now, while the PC is being built: it costs nothing and gets perception work started. Do not make it the car computer: it is ARM, so we would maintain a second build next to the x86 InCarPC fallback, and it gets no further JetPack updates.
